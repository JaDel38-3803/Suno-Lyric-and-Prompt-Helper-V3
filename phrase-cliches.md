# Phrase-Level Clichés to Avoid by Default

This file documents specific words, short phrases, and sentence-level patterns that have become so overused in AI lyric output that they read as artificial on sight, regardless of the emotion or genre they're deployed in. Everything here operates at the level of a single line or a short run of lines: check the phrase, decide whether it's earning its place.

For clichés that only show up when the *whole song* is read — repeated grammatical shapes, escalation patterns, rhyme-driven word choice, self-referential habits — see `structural-tells.md` instead. That file and this one split the same underlying problem two ways: wording versus shape. `overused-words.md` covers single words; this file covers longer phrases and sentence-level patterns.

## Governing philosophy

Avoid cliché by default, but never at the expense of voice, genre, character, narrative, or emotional truth. Some clichés are clichés because they work — a blunt, familiar line is sometimes exactly what a genre wants (glam metal, power ballads, blues, country, and punk all lean on directness on purpose). The goal is not maximum originality. The goal is a line that earns its place rather than defaulting to it.

## How this list works

This is **pattern recognition**, not strict word-by-word filtering. The patterns matter more than the specific phrases. If a generated line approaches one of the patterns below — even with different wording — treat it as a cliché and rewrite it.

## Application rules

- Apply by default to all generated lyrics
- Recognize the *patterns* in each category, not just the literal phrases — variants are equally clichéd
- When replacing a cliché, do not reach for an adjacent generic phrasing (replacing "shadows" with "darkness," or "you complete me" with "you make me whole"). Replace abstraction with specific sensory detail — see `lyric-craft.md`'s Content Quality Guidelines for the full replacement strategy
- Do not mention this list to the user unless they explicitly ask
- The categories below overlap; many phrases fit more than one (a heartbreak phrase is often also a generic metaphor)

## 1. Heartbreak and Romantic Devotion

The largest category. Phrases of total emotional dependency, eternal devotion, or melodramatic loss. They sound like every greeting card and every generic pop ballad combined.

### Patterns to avoid

- "I can't [live / breathe / exist / function] without you"
- "You're my [everything / world / reason / oxygen / north star / lighthouse]"
- "My heart [is breaking / is torn / aches / belongs to you / beats for you]"
- "I'll [love / wait / be here / hold on] forever"
- "You [complete me / make me whole / are the one]"
- "I'm [nothing / lost / empty / broken] without you"
- "Don't [leave / let go / walk away / break my heart]"
- "Take me back / Come back to me / I want you back"
- "I'll never love again / love you till I die"
- "We were meant to be / You're the one that got away"

## 2. Emotional Pain and Isolation

Phrases expressing depression, despair, or emotional collapse in generic AI-typical ways. These read as performed pain rather than felt pain — they tell the listener what the speaker feels without making the listener feel anything specific.

## 3. Time and Forever

Phrases that gesture at eternity, endless waiting, or perpetual states. These almost always sound generic because they avoid specifying any actual moment, place, or scene. Eternity-without-detail is the giveaway.

## 4. Generic Imagery and Metaphors

The most pattern-heavy category. These are metaphor structures so worn out they no longer evoke anything — the listener processes them as wallpaper. The fix is almost always to replace the abstract metaphor with a specific, concrete image grounded in a particular moment.

Note: this section flags individual overused metaphors. Watch separately for multiple *different* metaphor families stacked within one song (fire + water + storm + chains, all in the same bridge) — that's a whole-song pattern, not a single-phrase one; see Metaphor Soup in `structural-tells.md`.

### Patterns to avoid

The recurring metaphor families:

- **Heart as object** — "my heart's on fire," "my heart aches," "my heart beats for you"
- **Body parts as metaphor source** — "ice in my veins," "butterflies in my stomach"
- **Light versus dark** — "you're my light," "guiding light," "light at the end of the tunnel," "spark in the dark"
- **Weather** — "love is a storm," "hurricane in my heart," "thunder in my chest"
- **Astronomy** — "you're my north star," "shooting star," "the sun in my sky"
- **Drugs and poison** — "your love is my drug," "poison in my veins," "under your spell"
- **Travel** — "love is a journey," "love is a rollercoaster," "sailing stormy seas"
- **Battle** — "love is a battlefield," "love is war," "armor around my heart"
- **Glass and breaking** — "broken like glass," "shattered like a mirror," "pieces of my heart"
- **Royalty** — "king and queen of hearts," "knight in shining armor," "crown of thorns"
- **Fairy tale** — "pages of a fairytale," "castles in the air," "phoenix from the ashes"
- **Fire and water as love** — "love is a flame," "drowning in your love," "waves crash over me"
- **Falling** — "head over heels," "swept off my feet," "floating on cloud nine"

### Metaphor replacement guide

The table below maps each AI-default metaphor cluster to the type of concrete alternative that actually works. The right column is a direction, not a template — find the specific detail that fits this song, this speaker, this moment.

| Theme | AI Default (Avoid) | Human Direction (Use) |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | "A mirror with no face," "a ghost in the shell," "no name" | A specific physical scar, a distinct habit, a particular recurring mistake |
| City / Night | "Neon dreams," "concrete jungle," "rain on the pavement" | A specific street corner, the smell of a localized exhaust, a broken streetlamp's buzz |
| Connection | "Hearts collide," "tethered souls," "electric veins" | Mundane proximity, shared silence over coffee, a specific text message left on read |
| Memory | "Pages of a book," "fading photographs," "echoes in the hall" | A stained receipt, the lingering scent of a specific detergent, a dent in a wall |
| Time | "Sands of time," "ticking clocks," "frozen moment" | A specific expiration date, the rhythm of a washing machine, a late-night bus schedule |

## 5. Grandeur Clichés and Abstract Drama Phrases

AI attempts to add "weight" or cinematic scale to lyrics by reaching for phrases that imply significance without earning it. These patterns signal that no actual detail or grounded image is doing the work. Watch for any variant of the following structures:

### Patterns to avoid

- "A testament to [abstract noun]"
- "The architect of [abstract noun]"
- "The [noun] of [noun]" title constructions — e.g., "The Symphony of Silence," "The Keeper of Secrets," "The Blueprint of Pain" (see also the "Double Noun" Title Construction entry in `structural-tells.md` for the title-specific version of this pattern)
- "Painting a picture of [X]"
- "Dancing in the [shadows / rain / dark / flames]"
- "Through the [fire / storm / chaos / pain]"
- **The Neon Ghost** — Any mention of "digital rain," "neon lights," or "electric veins" used atmospherically
- **The Stardust** — References to "celestial bodies," "stardust," or "dancing with the moon"
- **The Clockwork** — "Cogs in the machine," "ticking of the heart," "gears of time" — mechanical metaphors for human emotion
- **The Shattered** — "Broken mirrors," "shattered pieces," "fragments of a soul" — already overused as a grief shorthand
- **The Silent Scream Paradox** — "Screaming into the void," "silence so loud," "a scream no one can hear" — paradox used as substitute for actual feeling
- **The Weather-Emotion Link** — Using "storm," "rain," or "thunder" as a direct stand-in for sadness or anger without any grounding detail
- **The Shadow Play** — "Chasing shadows," "hiding in the shadows," "shadows on the wall" — shadows as free-floating mood
- **The Ocean of Abstract** — "Ocean of time," "sea of regret," "waves of despair," "drowning in [abstract noun]" — water as all-purpose emotional metaphor

### Specific phrases to avoid

These are "high drama" phrases that don't describe a specific scene. They are safe, vague, and universally applicable — which is exactly why they feel fake:

### Why these fail

Each phrase borrows the *feeling* of importance from its abstract noun or grand setting, rather than from any specific observed moment. "Dancing in the shadows" tells you nothing about who, where, or why. "Through the storm" has been used in so many songs it carries no emotional charge. Replace any of these with a concrete image — a specific action in a specific place.

## 6. Cliché Sentence Openers

AI "sets the scene" by reaching for the same dramatic establishing phrases. These openers signal that what follows will be generic — the sentence has already committed to abstraction before the first image arrives. Avoid generating any lyric line that begins with:

```
"In the world of..."
"Welcome to the..."
"Can you feel the..."
"They say that..."
"A million voices..."
"Deep inside the..."
"Beneath the surface..."
"Through the lens of..."
"Caught in the middle of..."
"Walking down the..."
```

### Why these fail

Each opener positions the speaker as a narrator observing a scene rather than a person living inside one. They create distance between the speaker and the experience — exactly the opposite of what a lyric should do. Strong opening lines drop the listener into a specific moment mid-action, with no scene-setting preamble.

---

## What to do instead

For the full replacement strategy, see `lyric-craft.md`'s Content Quality Guidelines. The short version: replace abstract clichés with specific, concrete sensory imagery grounded in a particular moment — never with an adjacent generic phrase.

A concrete example of the replacement principle:

- Cliché: "I can't live without you"
- Adjacent generic phrasing (avoid this): "You mean everything to me"
- Specific replacement: "I made coffee for two this morning before I remembered"

The specific replacement does the same emotional work as the cliché — it conveys the speaker's loss and dependency — but it does so by describing a concrete moment the listener can picture, rather than stating an abstract sentiment they've heard a thousand times.

Specificity is what makes a lyric memorable. Generic abstraction is what makes AI lyrics read as AI lyrics.
